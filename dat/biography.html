I am a databases and data systems researcher focused on logging and record-keeping. I approach machine learning and AI from a data management perspective: what do teams need to record, how can they recover missing context, and how can those records support later review? Using AI in consequential work requires ongoing maintenance, oversight, and evidence that people can inspect. My research builds the data infrastructure to support that work. I combine systems research with qualitative studies of how teams work in practice, and co-design tools that fit their workflows.

During my Ph.D. at UC Berkeley, advised by Joe Hellerstein in the RISE Lab, I developed FlorDB, a logging and checkpointing database that lets developers query the context of past machine learning runs as dataframes, including information they did not think to log at the time (VLDB '21, CIDR '25). With Shreya Shankar, I also co-led an interview study of ML engineers across industries (CSCW '24). We found that production ML depends on substantial manual, iterative work and team knowledge. Engineers often struggle to explain what their models did and why, relying on constant monitoring, on-call rotations, handwritten filters, and hard-won institutional knowledge. These findings inform my work on systems that help teams preserve and retrieve the context needed to manage ML over time. My Ph.D. work was supported by an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship and a UC Berkeley Chancellor's Fellowship.

I'm currently a postdoc at Arizona State University's VADER Lab, advised by Ross Maciejewski. My recent work includes citation analysis and static checks of bulk academic submissions, as well as cost-benefit modeling of interactive ML systems (VIS '26). With Tyson Winarski at ASU's law school, I work on ScanStudio, which lets people digitize the papers and books they own and rely on, without cutting the bindings. With Ransalu Senanayake, I lead a contextual inquiry into logging practices in robotics labs, studying how teams record physical experiments that may be impossible to replay. My postdoctoral work is supported by a Presidential Postdoctoral Fellowship.
