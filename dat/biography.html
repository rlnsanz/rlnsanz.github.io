Hi, I'm Rolando Garcia, a data systems researcher and Presidential Postdoctoral Fellow at Arizona State University. I study how people answer new questions about experiments they've already run: what a team needs to record at the time, and what it can recover later. I observe and interview teams at work, and build record-keeping systems with them.

During my Ph.D. with Joe Hellerstein in UC Berkeley's RISE Lab, I developed FlorDB, a database for querying past machine learning (ML) runs. When users report a recurring mistake, how far back does it go? Engineers can add a check to today's code, and FlorDB replays earlier runs to show how often each model version made that mistake (VLDB '21, CIDR '25). With Shreya Shankar, I co-led an interview study showing how continual experimentation and collaboration sustain ML in production (CSCW '24). My Ph.D. was supported by an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship and a UC Berkeley Chancellor's Fellowship.

I now study the physical and social dimensions of context, from the conditions of a robot trial to the judgments people make as they write and review documents. At ASU's VADER Lab, I'm working with Ross Maciejewski on a tool that helps reviewers see how a manuscript's claims draw on cited sources and statistical evidence. I also helped develop a cost model for deciding when another round of feedback in interactive ML is worth the time and effort (VIS '26, Slides). With Ransalu Senanayake, I lead a field study of what robotics teams need to record to compare and reproduce trials. To study how knowledge workers check OCR text and verify corpora, ASU law professor Tyson Winarski and I are developing ScanStudio, a non-destructive alternative to bulk digitization.
